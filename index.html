Polyglot Watchlist

Find possible matches for a name across scripts and spellings, then let a human decide. Try it below — everything runs in your browser on fictional data.

Research prototype. Results are possible record matches for human review — never findings of identity, wrongdoing or sanctions status. Scores are uncalibrated similarity, not probabilities.

2 · How a search flows

Many ways to compare

Exact match, edit distance (RapidFuzz), sound-alike codes, transliteration to Latin, and a multilingual embedding model (E5). A hybrid mode fuses their ranked lists.

Same name ≠ same entity

Two different records can share one name. The system shows both and lets a person decide — it never merges or “clears” on its own.

Built to survive crashes

Every step is checkpointed. A crash mid-review resumes without losing evidence or re-running the search.

    4 · What the experiments showed

    Top-1 hits on small diagnostic sets (historical, regression-only — these examples were inspected during development, so they are not a measure of real-world accuracy).

    E5kept as the main retriever: it handled cross-script names far better than spelling alone.
    −an off-the-shelf reranker did not help — a negative result kept on record.
    4,500+automated tests across the repository, including crash-recovery and security checks.
